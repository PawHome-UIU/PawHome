SLIDE 9 - PROJECT OVERVIEW                                             (20 sec)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PawHome has three roles, and each one gets its own portal.

An adopter browses pets, takes the quiz, applies to adopt, and books
boarding.

Shelter staff list pets, review applications, log the daily care, and
record vaccinations.

An admin approves staff accounts, manages shelters, and runs reports.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 10 - SCOPE                                                       (23 sec)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

On the left is what we will build. Adoption from start to finish.
Boarding with daily care. The quiz. Health records. And role-based
access.

On the right is what we will not build. No online payment. No transport.
No mobile app. No veterinary advice.

Fees are recorded, but they are not collected. Payment is out of scope
on purpose.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 11 - TECHNOLOGY STACK                                            (20 sec)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

React on the frontend. Spring Boot with Java on the backend. MySQL 8.4
running in Docker. And JWT with BCrypt for security.

One thing to mention here. Every change to the database goes through a
Flyway migration file. So both of our laptops always run exactly the
same database.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 12 - THE DATABASE                                                (32 sec)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Now the database itself.

It is one MySQL schema. Twenty-seven tables and fifty-three foreign keys.

The whole design is built around two tables.

The first one is USERS. Twenty of the twenty-seven tables point back to
it. It holds adopters, shelter staff and admins - all in one table, with
a role column.

The second one is PETS. Nine tables point back to that.

Everything else sits in groups around those two. Shelters and boarding
suites. Traits and photos. The five quiz tables. Applications and
adoptions. And boarding, care logs and vaccinations.

On the right are the links to our draw.io diagrams.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 13 - SCHEMA AND NORMALISATION                                    (40 sec)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This slide is about what the database refuses to store.

On the left are the constraints. An email must be unique. A staff member
must belong to a shelter, and an adopter must not - that is a CHECK
constraint. Role and status are fixed lists, not free text.

On the right is normalisation. We are in third normal form. That means
one fact is written in one place only. A shelter's city lives on the
shelter table - it is not repeated on every pet.

We break that rule in five places, on purpose. Most are snapshots. The
applicant's address is copied when they apply, so the application still
shows where they lived at that time, even after they move house.

SLIDE 14 - THE ERD, PAGE BY PAGE                                  (about 2 min)
[Open docs/pawhome-erd.drawio now. Go through pages 1 to 6 in order.]
===============================================================================

--- PAGE 1 : THE WHOLE SCHEMA ---

This is the complete diagram. / It looks like a lot.

But it is organised around just two tables. [Point to users, then to
pets.] Twenty tables point back to USERS. Nine point back to PETS.

So the way to read it is not left to right. Find the two hubs, then
follow the lines outward. That is what I will do now.


--- PAGE 2 : IDENTITY AND SHELTERS ---

We start at the root of the whole design: SHELTERS.

This table has no foreign keys at all. Nothing sits above it. Every
other table eventually traces back here.

Next, USERS. This is the single most important table in the design.

The decision here is that we did NOT build three tables for three kinds
of people. No separate adopters table, no separate staff table. One
USERS table with a ROLE column - ADOPTER, STAFF or ADMIN. [Point to the
role row.]

The key attributes are:

  ID              - primary key
  EMAIL           - UNIQUE. This is what someone logs in with.
  ROLE            - which of the three kinds of account this is
  APPROVAL_STATUS - PENDING, APPROVED or REJECTED
  EMAIL_VERIFIED  - has this person confirmed their address
  IS_ACTIVE       - the kill switch
  SHELTER_ID      - nullable, because only staff belong to a shelter

The small table below is USER_TOKENS - email verification and password
reset. Look at the column name: TOKEN_HASH, not "token". We never store
the link we emailed, only a hash of it. So a leaked database is not a
stack of live "take over this account" links.


--- PAGE 3 : PETS, TRAITS AND HEALTH ---

PETS is the second hub.

The interesting column is OWNER_ID, and it is nullable for a real
reason. A pet here is one of two things. Either it is a shelter animal
waiting to be adopted - then it has no owner, so this is NULL. Or it is
somebody's own pet in for boarding - and then OWNER_ID points at that
person.

One table, two situations, separated by whether a column is NULL.

Also on this page is HEALTH_REMINDERS_SENT. It has a UNIQUE constraint
on the vaccination plus the reminder kind. That one constraint is what
makes sure an owner is emailed about a due vaccination exactly once, and
not every time the job runs.


--- PAGE 4 : THE MATCHING QUIZ ---

The quiz is five tables.

QUIZ_QUESTIONS holds the questions. QUIZ_OPTIONS holds the possible
answers to each question. QUIZ_ATTEMPTS is one person sitting the quiz
once. QUIZ_ANSWERS is what they picked.

And OPTION_TRAITS is the scoring rule - it says which traits each answer
counts towards. That is how we turn "I live in a small flat and I work
long hours" into a recommendation for a calm, independent cat.


--- PAGE 5 : ADOPTION ---

Two tables here, and the difference between them matters.

ADOPTION_APPLICATIONS is a request. Somebody applied. It may be
approved, it may be declined.

ADOPTIONS is the completed fact. The animal actually went home.

We kept them separate because an application is a conversation and an
adoption is a permanent record. Merging them would give us a table where
half the rows describe something that never happened.


--- PAGE 6 : BOARDING AND CARE ---

BOARDING_REQUESTS is the widest table in the schema. It carries six
foreign keys at once - the pet, the owner, the shelter, the suite, the
quiz attempt, and the staff member who reviewed it. A boarding booking
genuinely does touch six different things.

Hanging off it are BOARDING_INSTRUCTIONS, the owner's care notes.
BOARDING_UPDATES, the photo updates staff post back to the owner. And
CARE_TASKS and CARE_LOGS - what needs doing each day, and what actually
got done.

That is the whole schema, in six pages.

SLIDE 16 - RISKS AND DEPENDENCIES                                      (22 sec)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Three risks.

First, being a team of two. It will be tough to implement every feature,
but we will do our best.

Second, email is external. Verification and reminders depend on an
outside mail provider.

And third, schema drift. Every change must go through a migration file,
or our two databases stop matching.

One platform, from first browse to going home.

Thank you. We are happy to take your questions.

===============================================================================
LIKELY QUESTIONS
===============================================================================

  Q: Why one users table for three kinds of people?
  A: They share almost every column and all of the login logic. One table
     with a role column, plus a CHECK constraint, keeps them apart without
     copying everything three times.

  Q: Where is the many-to-many?
  A: Three places. Pets and traits, quiz options and traits, and users and
     pets for favourites. Each one is a junction table, and its primary key
     is the pair of foreign keys.

  Q: Why store the score on the quiz attempt?
  A: Because staff can edit the scoring weights later. If we recalculated
     it, an old attempt would silently change its result.

  Q: Which index, and why that one?
  A: Pets, on shelter id and status. That is the exact pair the catalogue
     filters on, so it is the query that runs most often.

  Q: Why is SHELTER_ID allowed to be NULL?
  A: Because only staff belong to a shelter. Every adopter has NULL there,
     and a CHECK constraint stops an adopter from claiming one.
